# What happens today

- You will be grouped into pairs randomly.
- You will prepare critique notes in `10 minutes`.
- Each student gives a `two-minute` critique.
- Tutor and peers respond with feedback.

<div class="mt-5 border-l-2 border-slate-400 pl-4 text-sm text-slate-600">
The goal is not to defend your work. The goal is to learn what to improve next.
</div>

<p class="text-xs uppercase tracking-[0.18em] text-slate-500">Participation</p>

# Late arrivals

We allow a `15 minute` buffer for late arrivals.

After the grouping stage:

- late students can only participate if they can form a new critique group
- most groups will have `2` students, but one final group of `3` is possible when numbers are odd
- we will not reshuffle the whole class once critique preparation has started
- once paired, exchange visualisations and begin preparing

> There is no catch-up critique session in consultation hours unless you have approved `special consideration`.

# During the 10-minute preparation

<VClicks>

1. inspect their partner's visualisation
2. use the critique rubric as the guide
3. identify the biggest issues first
4. prepare at least `four` ranked bullet points
5. focus on useful improvements, not minor cosmetic details

</VClicks>

---
layout: default
---

<p class="text-xs uppercase tracking-[0.18em] text-slate-500">What to say</p>

# Prepare these points

1. What is this visualisation trying to show?
2. What is the biggest issue?
3. Why does it matter?
4. How should it be improved?
5. What are the next most important fixes?

<div class="mt-5 border border-slate-300 bg-white/60 p-4 text-sm">
Look at: chart choice, misleading encodings, layout, typography, colour, and message clarity.
</div>

<p class="text-xs uppercase tracking-[0.18em] text-slate-500">Timing and presentation</p>

# Student presentation flow

- each student gives a `two-minute` critique
- keep comments ranked from most important to least important
- speak clearly and stay within time
- critique the design, not the person

::right::

# After your critique

- your tutor and classmates may respond
- listen for the main issue they want you to fix first
- use the discussion to improve your assignment, not just your presentation
- take notes while feedback is fresh
